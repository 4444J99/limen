# Charles's Downs Style voice: evidence sheet

This sheet explains how the archive becomes a reusable voice system. It distinguishes what the corpus **measures**, what editorial review **infers**, and what the new system **modernizes**. It does not treat historic product or health claims as factual authority.

## The voice in one sentence

Charles writes like a stylish friend who begins with what drew him in, notices the sensory and practical details, lets himself love the thing, admits the drawback, and lands on a personal verdict.

## Quantitative fingerprint

The causal baseline is 257 posts published from 2017 through 2024: 76,597 alphabetic tokens, a 265-word median post, a 20.95-word mean sentence, and a 23-word median paragraph.

| Signal | Baseline result | Editorial consequence |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| First-person singular | 3,448 uses; 45.01 per 1,000 words | Write as `I` when Charles is speaking. |
| First-person plural | 75 uses; 0.98 per 1,000 words | `We` is exceptional, not the house default. |
| Second person | 734 uses; 9.58 per 1,000 words | Help the reader choose without turning every sentence into advice. |
| `I love` | 154 uses | Visible enthusiasm is authentic when attached to a specific reason. |
| `My favorite` | 92 uses | Personal ranking is part of the voice, not a neutral-review defect. |
| `However` | 180 uses | Praise normally makes room for a qualification. Vary the transition in polished copy. |
| `Overall` | 124 uses; `overall I` appears 75 times | End in a personal conclusion rather than an impersonal summary. |
| Shopping cue | 178 of 257 posts | Commerce is historically native, but modern links need a live check and disclosure. |
| Reader thanks | 88 of 257 posts | Warm signoffs are established, though they fade sharply in later years. |
| Numeric rating | 35 of 257 posts | Ratings are available but not required; a use-case verdict is more durable. |
| First-person title | 28 of 257 posts | First-person framing is established in titles as well as article bodies. |
| Review in title | 86 of 257 posts | Direct, searchable review framing is historically grounded. |
| Question title | 6 of 257 posts | Questions should be purposeful, not a default headline trick. |
| Question marks | 0.21 per 1,000 words | Avoid faux-conversational strings of questions. |
| Exclamation marks | 0.52 per 1,000 words | Charles's enthusiasm lives in opinion words, not punctuation volume. |

The perspective evidence is unusually strong: first-person singular appears about **46 times** as often as first-person plural. A generic `we/our` brand voice would erase the most stable historical feature in the archive.

## Recurrent narrative movement

Cross-category review found a dependable emotional sequence:

1. **Discovery or desire.** A friend, store, trip, need, routine, or visual detail creates the reason to care.
2. **Direct notice.** Charles names how the product feels, smells, fits, looks, behaves, or changes the ritual.
3. **Enthusiasm.** He permits words such as love, favorite, amazing, and obsessed when the feeling is earned.
4. **Qualification.** A price comparison, fit issue, ingredient concern, inconvenience, or mismatch interrupts the praise.
5. **Verdict.** `Overall` often returns the article to Charles's own decision.
6. **Invitation.** A shop cue or reader thanks closes the relationship.

That arc is an editorial inference supported by the frequency of personal language, qualification markers, verdict markers, shopping cues, signoffs, and ratings. It is more useful than copying any single sentence.

## The archive changed over time

| Era | What the measurements show | How to use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2017–2018 foundation | 94 posts; first-person singular 57.12 per 1,000 words; 70 shopping cues; 27 numeric ratings | Strongest evidence for Charles's personal, enthusiastic baseline. Preserve the point of view, not the long paragraphs or rough mechanics. |
| 2019–2020 review engine | 76 posts; 49 review titles; `however` 5.03 per 1,000 words; 53 reader-thanks endings | Strongest evidence for the praise → caveat → verdict review rhythm. |
| 2021–2023 expansion | 63 posts; first-person singular falls to 30.88 while second person rises to 11.06; median paragraph drops to 10.5 words | Useful evidence for shorter modules and reader utility, but less pure as an author fingerprint. |
| 2024 editorial | 24 posts; median length rises to 343 words; first-person singular falls to 20.78; second person rises to 13.01 | Evidence of a reported/SEO-facing channel evolution. Keep the added context, but restore Charles's personal center. |
| 2026 rose-water post | One post, excluded from the baseline | Evaluation material only; it cannot validate the system that helped produce it. |

The modern house style should therefore combine the early personal center with the later structural clarity: Charles's `I`, one thought per paragraph, stronger sourcing, and none of the inherited run-ons or generic SEO padding.

## What is evidence, and what is a guardrail

| Class | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observed | Reproducible corpus measurement | `I` greatly exceeds `we`; 178 posts contain shopping language. |
| Inferred | A pattern found by cross-era, cross-category editorial review | Personal hook → sensory evidence → qualification → verdict. |
| Modernized | A deliberate improvement, not an imitation claim | One thought per paragraph; clean syntax; restrained repetition. |
| Prohibited | A truth, safety, or trust boundary | No invented use, unsupported medical promise, or undisclosed paid link. |

The archive is evidence of **voice**, not a source of current factual truth. Before publication, every material, ingredient, safety, care, performance, health, sustainability, price, and availability claim needs current support. A manufacturer page can establish what a brand says; it does not independently prove the claim. Charles's personal experience must come from Charles, never from the assembler.

## Editorial acceptance test

A new Downs Style draft passes the voice gate when:

- the speaker is recognizably Charles, using `I` unless a real team action requires `we`;
- the opening gives a human reason to care before giving a definition;
- praise is attached to a concrete detail and challenged by at least one honest limitation when relevant;
- sections react to one another instead of forming a catalogue;
- the conclusion makes a use-case decision rather than repeating the list;
- paragraphs carry one thought and the syntax is clean;
- factual claims are sourced independently of the historic corpus;
- no personal use or result is invented; and
- commerce is current, transparent, and disclosed close to the link.
