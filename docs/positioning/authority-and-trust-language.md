# Authority and trust language

This W06 copy set makes authority legible without making it territorial. It is
an authorial, deterministic language check only; W07 owns target-reader review.
It does not create authority, change a mandate, grant access, or publish an
offer.

## Core authority boundary

I bring **additive leverage** to a problem the organization already owns. My
**sponsor-granted scope** is explicit in a written mandate. I work through
**collaboration**, keep **reversible work** reversible until a named owner
accepts the evidence, preserve **current-owner visibility**, and end with a
clear **handoff** and return path.

The method is evidence-first: name the decision, the owner, the evidence that
can close it, the access boundary, and the next reversible step. Do not imply
that specialist support grants organizational control.

## Threat triggers and evidence-first reframes

| Threat trigger | What creates the response | Evidence-first reframe | Boundary to state plainly |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Control-loss fear | A systems operator sounds like a replacement for current leadership. | The contribution is a bounded decision and operating improvement for a named mandate. | The sponsor grants scope; current owners keep their roles and decision rights. |
| Access and custody fear | Broad curiosity or unspecified access can sound like observation for a separate agenda. | Access is limited to the evidence needed for the agreed question and is recorded by purpose. | Ask before a new question, source, system, or access boundary is added. |
| Irreversibility fear | A proposed operating change appears permanent before its evidence is tested. | Start with a reversible step, a test, and an owner-approved acceptance condition. | Roll back a failed change and preserve the decision record. |
| Role-displacement fear | “End-to-end” language can imply that the team becomes subordinate or unnecessary. | Make interfaces, ownership, and verification clearer so the existing team can operate the result. | Do not seek hiring, reporting, reorganization, or daily-operations authority. |
| Motivation-conflict fear | Independent work can be read as a competing obligation. | The stated role or engagement is the agenda; independent work is method evidence, not a competing mandate. | Raise a material change early and keep confidential material inside agreed controls. |
| Status threat | Large claims can sound like a bid for rank rather than a contribution to a decision. | Lead with a bounded problem, observable evidence, and a useful next decision. | Do not imply executive standing, dominance, or organizational ownership. |

## Authority and answer library

### How does your work fit with existing leadership?

I bring additive leverage to the mandate the team already owns. I make the
decision path, evidence, and interfaces clearer so current owners can act with
more confidence; I do not make their ownership less visible.

### What authority are you asking for?

I accept sponsor-granted scope in a written mandate: one named decision, the
minimum agreed access, and a defined acceptance path. Anything outside that
scope returns to the sponsor before work proceeds.

### How will you use access?

I use only the access needed to answer the agreed question, record its purpose,
and ask before a new question or access boundary is added. The resulting
evidence belongs in the organization’s decision record.

### How do you work with the team?

I work in collaboration with the people who operate the system. Their domain
knowledge and current constraints shape the work; my contribution is to make
decision rights, verification, cost boundaries, and handoff explicit.

### What happens if the proposed change is wrong?

I begin with reversible work, a visible test, and an owner-approved acceptance
condition. If the evidence does not support the change, we roll it back, retain
the decision record, and choose the next bounded step together.

### What remains after the work ends?

The goal is current-owner visibility, usable evidence, and a clean handoff. The
named internal owner receives the decision record, operating instructions,
rollback information, and the next review point.

### Are you proposing a new executive layer?

I offer specialist support for a named decision, not a substitute for the
organization’s leadership. The useful measure is whether the team can make and
own better decisions after the work is handed over.

### Why should a client or hiring team trust the scope?

The scope is visible in the mandate, the evidence, the acceptance condition,
and the handoff. I state what the public proof establishes and its limits
rather than converting method evidence into a claim about a buyer’s outcome.

## Language guardrails

Prefer:

- “I will make the decision path and evidence legible with the current owners.”
- “The sponsor grants the scope; any expansion returns for approval.”
- “We can test this reversibly and preserve the return path.”
- “The result should be operable by the named internal owner.”
- “Here is the evidence, its limit, and the next decision it supports.”

Do not use:

- language that promises to take over, replace leadership, own the organization,
  bypass approval, or become an executive substitute;
- language that suggests undisclosed observation, extraction of unrelated
  information, or access beyond the agreed question;
- language that makes a permanent change before evidence and owner acceptance;
- evasive reassurance without naming scope, evidence, owner, and return path.

## Deterministic scoring rubric

This rubric evaluates the answer library, not a human reader’s reaction. Each
positive signal is worth one point; each defect is worth minus five points.
The library clears the pre-W07 standard at eight or more points and zero
defects.

| Signal | Evidence required in the answer library |
| --- | --- |
| Additive contribution | `additive leverage` |
| Mandate discipline | `sponsor-granted scope` and `written mandate` |
| Collaboration | `collaboration` |
| Reversibility | `reversible work` and `roll it back` |
| Owner respect | `current-owner visibility` |
| Handoff | `clean handoff` |
| Access discipline | `only the access needed` and `ask before` |
| Evidence-first decision making | `evidence` and `acceptance condition` |

| Defect | Forbidden in the answer library |
| --- | --- |
| Territorial posture | `take over`, `own the organization`, `bypass approval` |
| Evasion | `just trust me`, `don’t worry`, `no questions asked` |
| Surveillance posture | `surveil`, `spy on`, `monitor everybody` |
| Executive substitution | `replace leadership`, `executive substitute` |

## Rollback

Withdraw any reframe that cannot state a sponsor, scope, evidence, owner, and
return path. Return to evidence-first plain language; do not compensate with
larger authority, stronger status language, or broader access.
